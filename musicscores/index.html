Music Scores by Canonical Volume

Select a volume link below to explore the underlying musical assets. The music scores are systematically catalogued across two distinct formats: .mscz files optimized for direct manipulation in MuseScore, and universal .xml trees for compatibility across alternative notation software suites.

Each directory contains its own optimized JSON structure and a high-density, color-coded visual map tracking the exact melodic contours and shapes of each chapter—allowing you to inspect the contoured architecture of the music at a glance.

Nevi'im (Prophets)

Ketuvim (Writings)

  • The Psalms • Proverbs • The Book of Job • The Five Scrolls • Daniel, Ezra, Nehemiah • Chronicles

From any of the chapters you can highlight a motif and search for it in the rest of the canonical text. Click a row to see recurring phrases in the chapter and to engage the search button in the navigation block for wider distribution of the motif. Motifs are mapped from 3 to 5 notes, so if there is no result, shorten or lengthen your selection.

There is a rather large file containing all the shapes of phrases, approaching the atnah, and returning to the tonic, and verses without a subdominant. You can search by hand in this file using your browser search facility. All these tools allow us to see and imagine what musical language the text requires as seen by the originators of the te'amim. The file comes from a trace I included in the Unicode to MusicXML program written c 2013. It has over 730,000 rows, at least one row for every syllable in Tanach. It does not have rows for the very small number of syllables with two sublinear accents under one letter, fewer than 20 in 730,000, most but not all in the decalogues. The JSON trace files do not reflect the second sublinear accent. They are evident in the music or the Hebrew that accompanies it.
